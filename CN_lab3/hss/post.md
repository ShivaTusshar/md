## 12. Casanova – "Private and Public Religions" (1992)
**Core puzzle:** Modernity says "religion is a private affair" (freedom of conscience = first freedom; religion pushed out of state, economy, science) — yet in the 1980s religion went **public** worldwide. Casanova calls this **"deprivatization"** of religion.

- **Four global cases** that triggered the reassessment: Islamic Revolution in Iran, Solidarity & Catholicism in Poland, Protestant Fundamentalism in US politics, Catholicism in Nicaragua/Latin America
- **Theory of secularization** has a core = **differentiation** (state, economy, science "emancipate" from religious norms; religion confined to private sphere). He accepts this structural trend has huge "directional inertia"
- Critics' mistake: pointing to people still believing in God doesn't refute secularization theory; the real challenge is religion **refusing the private role** assigned to it
- **Private/public is a binary that fails** — modernity is actually **tripartite: family – civil society – state** (Hegel); "the social" sphere sits *between* public and private
- Weintraub's **4 models of public/private**: (1) liberal-economistic (state vs market) (2) republican-virtue (political citizenship) (3) Ariès-type (fluid sociability) (4) feminist/economic-history (family vs market)
- **Not a "return of the sacred"** — that's a "red herring." Questioning privatization only means religions can keep playing a public role: raising **moral/normative questions** about the autonomous spheres (market, state) — e.g., Catholic bishops on nuclear arms and capitalism
- Religion as **counterfactual normative critique** of modernity (like feminism, civic-republican critiques)
- **Cyclical revival theories** (Bell, Stark-Bainbridge: need for meaning/compensation) fail — revival happened in Poland, USA, Brazil, not in the most secularized (Sweden, France)
- **Islam** (Umma = religious + political community) and the myth of origins make differentiation harder; colonialism/collapse of Ottoman empire opened space for religious-political experiments
- **Key takeaway:** the thesis of **privatization** (not differentiation) is what's challenged; history is "still open"

## 13. Srivastava – "Urban Spaces, Disney-Divinity and Moral Middle Classes in Delhi" (2009)
**Argument:** New urban spaces in Delhi are **making a "moral middle class"** through consumption, religiosity and a particular relationship with the state. Two sites: **Akshardham Temple complex** and the **Bhagidari scheme**.

**Akshardham (BAPS Swaminarayan sect, completed 2005, on Yamuna bank)**
- Hi-tech "religious theme park": IMAX show, animatronic Hall of Values, boat ride through "10,000 years of Indian history," musical fountain, five-star-hotel lobby, McDonald's-like food hall, shop with baseball caps
- **Inspired by Disneyland/Universal Studios**; emphasis on **time-management, efficiency, technology**
- Only Hindu/"ancient" themes — **no Muslim representation**
- Built beside demolished **Nangla Machi slum** (jhuggi colony) — "clean" spaces made as "unclean" ones removed (53 JJ colonies demolished 2000–06; ~79,000 families) — **legality/illegality** double standard: BAPS's "clout" erases "encroachment"
- Visitors: non-English speaking, extended families, many women — **not** the upper-middle class → a *particular fraction* of the middle class

**Key concepts**
- **Surplus consumption**: consuming across many "glancing" timespans/contexts; collapsing time & space; refusing to consume "rationally" (rushing to next show, little interest in the "message")
- **Moral consumption**: active consumerism accompanied by **anxiety about "Indianness"**
- **"Retractable modernity"**: moral middle class can engage global consumerism AND "pull back" to tradition — unlike a "deracinated/westernised" class that only consumes capitalism's products
- **Threshold space**: Akshardham is *in the midst of* modernity (highways, malls, flyovers), not nostalgic retreat
- Link to **Bourdieu's distinctions** (class politics) and **Chatterjee's** nationalist inner/outer (spiritual/material) resolution
- Especially accounts for **women as new consumers** — secure in ability to withdraw to family/"true Indianness"

**Bhagidari scheme (Delhi govt, 2000; "citizen–government partnership")**
- Residents' Welfare Associations (RWAs) + Market Traders' Associations + officials in 3-day workshops (run by NGO ACORD, based on corporate "Large Group Interactive Event" with "sleep research" claims)
- Produces a **version of citizenship**: authorised colonies, transparency, cooperation — but also **exclusion**: surveillance of servants, "verification," treating JJ dwellers as "security threats"
- **Bhagidari Utsav** at Pragati Maidan (trade-fair ground) — carnival atmosphere, baseball caps, flags, song to "Old MacDonald" tune
- **Consuming family = moral fulcrum** of citizenship; state, market, religious sect converge

**Takeaway:** Akshardham + Bhagidari = **one conjoined topography of moral middle-classness**.

## 14. Ambedkar – "Castes in India: Their Mechanism, Genesis and Development" (1916)
**Core thesis:** **Endogamy is the *only* essential characteristic of caste.** Explaining how endogamy is maintained = explaining caste.

- **Critique of earlier definitions:** Senart (pollution, close corporation), Nesfield (no inter-dining), Risley, Ketkar (prohibition of intermarriage + membership by birth). Ambedkar: pollution is a *religious flavour*, not essential; Nesfield mistakes **effect for cause**; Ketkar's two traits = **two sides of the same coin**
- **Caste is "an artificial chopping off" of a homogeneous population** (India has cultural unity) — unlike mere tribal/racial separation (e.g., US)
- **Superposition of endogamy on exogamy** = creation of caste (gotra exogamy already existed)
- **Surplus man and surplus woman problem**: in a closed group, equal sex ratio is needed; death of spouse creates "surplus" who threatens endogamy
- **Four means** to solve it (these *create* caste): (1) **Sati** (burning widow) (2) **Enforced widowhood** (3) **Imposing celibacy on widower** (4) **Girl marriage**
- Customs were **"honoured because they were practised"** — philosophies came later to justify/"gild the pill"; means idealized as ends
- **"A Caste is an Enclosed Class"**; society is always composed of classes — rejects the "atomistic individual" as "humbug"
- **Genesis:** Brahmins first enclosed themselves (practised sati, widowhood, girl marriage); non-Brahmin groups **imitated** them — "the infection of imitation" (Tarde) → they became castes
- **Manu did not create caste** (caste pre-dates him; he codified/upheld it); **Brahmins did not "create"** it either — spreading it was beyond their power; it spread by imitation
- Caste is thus a **mechanism of closure**, not mainly religion/race/function/occupation (rejects Nesfield's occupation theory as circular — *petitio principii*)

## 15. Iversen & Raghavendra – "What the Signboard Hides: Food, Caste and Employability in Small South Indian Eating Places" (2006)
**Question:** In a sector built on **purity/pollution** (food prep & serving), does caste still decide who gets jobs? Study of **Udupi hotels** (small vegetarian eateries; employing mostly male migrants from coastal Karnataka) — case studies in villages, small towns, Bangalore, Mumbai + 60 worker interviews.

- **Commensality** (inter-dining) = "root of all caste distinction" (Conlon)
- Udupi hotels began Brahmin-owned (Bangalore ~1900; Mumbai late 1930s); signboards once said "only for Brahmins" — caste now signalled **covertly** (names like Pai, Adiga; Krishna Bhavan etc.)
- **Brahmin ownership = commercial advantage** (food acceptable to all twice-born castes) **but orthodoxy can become a liability** as tastes change (e.g., Darbar hotel owner lost a Brahmin customer over omelettes)
- Expansion in Mumbai driven by **Bunts** taking over Irani cafés — not Brahmins
- **Job ladder:** start as **cleaner** → counter/supplier → cook. Kitchen = highest pollution-sensitivity
- **Findings:**
  - **Village hotels**: tradition holds — Brahmin-owned, Brahmin cooks; non-Brahmins rarely in kitchen
  - **Towns/cities**: caste barriers **loosen** — mixed-caste cooks; commercial pragmatism wins
  - **Cleaners** are now "others" (northern Karnataka Kurubas, Biharis); **Nepalese** cooks for Chinese/North Indian dishes
  - **Deception/tension** between signboard (Brahminical ideal) and practice — e.g., **Billava chief cook who "resembled a Brahmin"**; suppliers from middle castes wear Brahminical attire (even adopt Brahmin names)
  - **SCs** get into kitchen "through the backdoor" only by **concealing caste** (Raghu and Umesh examples)
  - Brahmins' "pucca" workers disappeared in some hotels (last in 1970s); Muslims/Christians never in orthodox Bangalore hotel despite 25% Muslim customers
- **Theory debate:** Dumont (purity/pollution as immutable principle) vs **Srinivas** (fluid ranking, Sanskritisation) vs Fuller (critique of Dumont's rigidity) → evidence supports **persistence + change**, context-dependent
- **Takeaway:** Caste-based exclusion persists most where **ownership is Brahmin & the market is local/static**; weakens where **market competition and migrant labour** dominate — but caste remains something to be **hidden or performed** (hence "signboard hides")
